Kinematics Inside V-JEPA 2

Where does a frozen video encoder represent a moving disk's direction, speed and acceleration, how many dimensions does each take, and can it be steered? We reproduce the probing, nullspace and steering methods of Joseph et al. (arXiv 2602.07050) on the supplied synthetic clips. Layer-wise probing, nullspace probing and direction steering were each run twice: once with our own protocol, and once with the paper's protocol as literally as its text allows. The propagation and confound analyses use our protocol only.

Summary

  • A
    All three variables are linearly decodable from the first transformer block. At our layer 1, our ridge probe gets test R² 0.85 for direction and 0.98 for speed and acceleration. The paper's protocol on the same data gets 0.66, 0.88 and 0.82 at that block. A randomly initialised V-JEPA gets 0.83, 0.93 and 0.91, so on these clips one attention block over mean-pooled tokens already exposes the motion.
  • B
    Direction shows no step at the paper's layer 8. The paper's pooled direction curve jumps from about 0.5 to 0.93 at its layer 8 (Fig. 2c). On our data, direction error falls smoothly: 12° at our layer 1, 4.3° at layer 9, 3.1–3.6° from layer 13 on. Low early scores like the paper's appear only with very little data and a gradient-trained probe that has not converged. With 96 clips that probe gets R² 0.31 at layer 1, or 0.57 with 10× more epochs, against 0.63 for ridge. Even then the rise is complete by our layer 6, three blocks before the paper's layer 8.
  • C
    Each variable spans tens of dimensions. With our protocol at layer 12, R² falls below 0.3 after removing 58 dimensions for direction, 51 for speed and 48 for acceleration. With the paper's protocol at its layer 8, the stopping rule is hit after a median of 60 direction probes (120 dimensions) and 44 speed probes, against the paper's ≈44 and ≈28. Removing random directions does nothing; removing the top 4–13 principal components collapses decoding.
  • D
    Paper-style steering reproduces at the layer where it is applied. Averaged over three train/test splits, the error to a 90° target falls from 76° with 1 probe to 8° with 20 probes (6° at 224 px). The paper reports 11.9°. The result depends on the split: 3.5–14° at 20 probes.
  • E
    Downstream, the steering mostly fades. We added the same edit to every token at our layer 12 and let the network run on. Direction error to target then goes 4° → 25° → 45° → 73° within three blocks, ending at 85° (a random edit gives 90°). Speed and acceleration keep part of the effect. The edit is not specially targeted. Along its own direction, the speed and acceleration edits decay like a real clip-to-clip difference. The direction edit decays like a random edit for three blocks, then levels off between the two.
  • F
    "Acceleration" is mostly displacement. We applied the acceleration probe to constant-velocity clips, whose true acceleration is 0. It predicts 6.8 m/s² on average and tracks how far the disk travelled with r = 0.993. The paper's datasets are built the same way.

FEATURES

Model and features

The encoder is facebook/vjepa2-vitl-fpc64-256, kept frozen. The predictor is never used. The checkpoint was trained on 64-frame clips; here it sees 16 frames.

Preprocessing
The HF processor's default resizes to 292 px and centre-crops to 256, which would cut about 6% off each border and could clip the disk. The clips are already 256², so we only rescale and normalise.
224-px variant
The paper's App. C.6 describes 224×224 input (1,568 tokens). We ran every paper-protocol experiment at both 256 px (native checkpoint) and 224 px (bicubic resize of the full frame). The HF implementation derives the RoPE position grid from the config (16×16), not from the input, so at 224 px we set it to 14×14 on every layer. Without that fix, token positions are scrambled; our first 224-px run had this bug.
Controls
A randomly initialised V-JEPA 2 with the same architecture (HF default initialisation); raw pixels (red channel, 32×32 per frame, linear); the disk's centroid trajectory and visible-area fraction, with degree-2 polynomial features.

How clean is the paper's split?

Only partly. In layer-wise probing, the literal reading of App. B picks the best of 20 configurations on the same fold it reports. In practice the inflation is small here: the nested version is at most 0.028 lower. Nullspace probing has no separate test set. In steering, the steering subspace comes from train, but the evaluation probe is trained on the test clips it then reads.

Why does direction look earlier here than in the paper?

We changed two things at a time, probe type and data regime, on the direction set. "Paper-like" keeps only constant-velocity clips at the paper's 8 directions (0°, 45°, …, 315°), which is 96 clips. That is a quarter of the paper's 392, so it exaggerates the effect of little data. All four probe types use 5-fold CV with hyper-parameters chosen on an inner split.

Reading the paper directly

App. C.5, p. 26: "direction-related signals are already present in early layers, but remain tightly coupled to specific spatial locations … Mean-pooled probes can nevertheless achieve modest performance by linearly combining these fragmented signals … per-patch probe performance rises abruptly at the emergence zone, while mean-pooled performance improves more gradually."

"More gradually" is relative to the per-patch probes. The paper's pooled curves still step at layer 8: Fig. 2c goes from about 0.5 to 0.93, and Fig. 18a's pooled curve from about 0.47 to 0.85. We see no such step, so this is a real difference between the two setups. Per-patch probing is the next test.

EXPERIMENT 3

Multi-probe subspace steering

The first N nullspace probes span a subspace V. For a clip's pooled activation x, the coordinates inside V are replaced by the values that make every probe read the target, and everything outside V is kept (App. C.12, Eq. 8). We evaluate in two places: at the same layer, as the paper does, and after letting the remaining blocks run.

A · Same-layer read-out

In no split did the paper's steering-probe sequence reach R² < 0.1 within 25 probes; the lowest was 0.80–0.83 at 256 px. The paper reports reaching it.

Steering is specific. Steering speed or acceleration leaves the θ read-out of the same clips unchanged up to about 20 probes (5.2° clean vs 5.6° steered for speed). At 100 probes it degrades, to 17.6° for speed and 33.8° for acceleration, because the subspace starts to take in shared dimensions.

CONTROL

Displacement confound

The speed set has no acceleration and the acceleration set always starts from rest. Within each set, the distance travelled over the clip determines the label, so a probe can succeed by reading where the disk ends up. To test this, we applied the layer-12 acceleration probe to the speed clips, whose true acceleration is 0.

Acceleration probe applied to constant-velocity clipsValue
Mean predicted acceleration (true value 0)6.76 m/s²

The probe reads distance travelled. The paper builds its datasets the same way, so its claims about acceleration face the same confound. The direction set mixes constant-velocity and accelerating clips over overlapping distances, which allows a direct test: can the representation tell "accelerating" from "constant velocity" when distance is matched?

NEXT

Limits and next steps

  • Per-patch probes. This is where the paper's appendix places its sharpest transition (Fig. 18a, c), and we have not run it yet.
  • Training-set size sweep. Probe R² by layer at 77, 150, 314 and 600 training clips would show directly how much early-layer decodability depends on data.
  • Acceleration test with distance matched. Use the mixed direction set to classify motion type when distance travelled is matched.
  • Stronger steering. Add Δ only to disk tokens, re-apply at every layer, or steer later in the network, to find an edit that later probes read.
  • Variance. Paper-protocol steering varies strongly with the train/test split (3.5–14° at 20 probes), and nullspace counts vary by about ±5–10 probes across seeds. Our own protocol uses one split; more splits would tighten the numbers.
  • Differences from the paper that remain. Our stimulus is flat 2D (theirs is a shaded 3D sphere on a textured floor); we use 64 directions (theirs 8); some protocol details are unspecified (epochs, batch size, feature scaling, CV groups, layer indexing). Paper values were read off plots and are approximate. The paper's own counts are inconsistent: Fig. 22 gives about 44 direction probes at layer 8, and Table 3 gives 136 dimensions (68 probes).
