Kinematics Inside V-JEPA 2

Where a frozen video encoder represents a moving disk's direction, speed and acceleration, how many dimensions each uses, and whether the representation can be steered. We reproduce the probing, nullspace and steering methods of Joseph et al. (arXiv 2602.07050) on the supplied clips, with our own protocol and with the paper's.

Summary

  • All three variables are linearly decodable from the first transformer block (R² 0.85 / 0.98 / 0.98). A randomly initialised encoder gets 0.83 / 0.93 / 0.91.
  • Direction has no step at the paper's layer 8. Error falls smoothly from 12° (layer 1) to 3° (layer 13+). The paper's low early-layer scores appear only with little data and an under-trained gradient probe.
  • Each variable occupies about 50–60 dimensions at layer 12. Redundancy drops sharply between layers 7 and 8.
  • Paper-style steering reproduces at the steered layer: 76° → 8° error to target from 1 to 20 probes (paper: 11.9°).
  • Propagated through the remaining blocks, the edit fades like any other offset. Direction ends at 85° error (random edit: 90°); speed and acceleration keep part of the effect.
  • The acceleration probe reads displacement: on constant-velocity clips it predicts 6.8 m/s² and tracks distance travelled with r = 0.993.

Data and model

Three sets of 16-frame, 256×256 clips at 24 fps: direction (1,500 clips, 64 angles), speed (1,536, 0.25–4 m/s) and acceleration (1,536, 0.25–10 m/s², from rest). Labels match the disk's measured trajectory (32.0 px/m). The disk decodes as orange, not blue as documented.

Encoder: facebook/vjepa2-vitl-fpc64-256, frozen, no predictor. Frames are rescaled and ImageNet-normalised, with no resize or crop. Features are the residual stream after each of the 24 blocks (layer 0 = patch embedding), mean-pooled over all 2,048 tokens. Splits are 60/20/20 by label value; test is used only for reported numbers.

Results

Limits and next steps

  • Per-patch probes: where the paper places its sharpest transition (Fig. 18); not run.
  • Training-set-size sweep at each layer.
  • Acceleration vs constant velocity with distance travelled matched, using the mixed direction set.
  • Steering variants: disk tokens only, re-applied at every layer, or later layers.
  • Our protocol uses one split; paper-protocol steering varies 3.5–14° across splits.
  • Remaining differences from the paper: flat 2D disk vs shaded 3D sphere; 64 directions vs 8.
