Kinematics Inside V-JEPA 2

Where a frozen video encoder represents a moving disk's direction, speed and acceleration, how many dimensions each uses, and whether the representation can be steered. We reproduce the probing, nullspace and steering methods of Joseph et al. (arXiv 2602.07050) on the supplied clips, with our own protocol and with the paper's.

Summary

  • All three variables are linearly decodable from the first transformer block (R² 0.85 / 0.98 / 0.98). A randomly initialised encoder gets 0.83 / 0.93 / 0.91.
  • Direction has no step at the paper's layer 8. Pooled error falls smoothly from 12° (layer 1) to 3° (layer 13+). Per-patch probes go from median R² 0.55 (layer 1) to 0.95 (layer 6), three blocks earlier than the paper's transition. The paper's low early-layer scores appear only with little data and an under-trained gradient probe.
  • Each variable occupies about 50–60 dimensions at layer 12. Redundancy drops sharply between layers 7 and 8.
  • Paper-style steering reproduces at the steered layer: 76° → 8° error to target from 1 to 20 probes (paper: 11.9°). Steering to label values never seen by any probe is as accurate as to seen values.
  • Propagated through the remaining blocks, the edit fades like any other offset. Direction ends at 85° error (random edit: 90°); speed and acceleration keep part of the effect.
  • The acceleration probe reads displacement: on constant-velocity clips it predicts 6.8 m/s² and tracks distance travelled with r = 0.993.

Data and model

Three sets of 16-frame, 256×256 clips at 24 fps: direction (1,500 clips, 64 angles), speed (1,536, 0.25–4 m/s) and acceleration (1,536, 0.25–10 m/s², from rest). Labels match the disk's measured trajectory (32.0 px/m). The disk decodes as orange, not blue as documented.

Encoder: facebook/vjepa2-vitl-fpc64-256, frozen, no predictor. Frames are rescaled and ImageNet-normalised, with no resize or crop. Features are the residual stream after each of the 24 blocks (layer 0 = patch embedding), mean-pooled over all 2,048 tokens. Splits are 60/20/20 by label value; test is used only for reported numbers.

Results

Additional checks

  • Probe type and data size (direction, layer 1, 5-fold CV): ridge 0.87; paper-style gradient probe 0.66, or 0.72 with 10× the epochs. On a 96-clip, 8-direction subset like the paper's: ridge 0.63, gradient probe 0.31 (0.57 with 10× epochs). Low early-layer scores need little data and an under-trained probe. In every setting the rise is complete by layer 6.
  • Held-out target values: with every 4th label value withheld from all probes, steering held-out clips to unseen values is as accurate as to seen values (4.3° vs 4.3°; 0.088 vs 0.088 m/s; 0.23 vs 0.23 m/s² at 10 probes).
  • Persistence of the edit downstream (component along Δ at layer 24): steering edit 0.07–0.10; a real clip-to-clip difference 0.08–0.11; a random edit of equal norm 0.02. The network attenuates offsets in general; it does not single out the steering edit.
  • Displacement confound: within each set, distance travelled is fixed by the label. The acceleration probe applied to constant-velocity clips (true acceleration 0) predicts 6.8 m/s² on average and tracks distance travelled with r = 0.993. The paper's datasets have the same structure.
  • The paper's sawtooth (Fig. 4c): under its protocol our accuracy-within-15° curves are jagged too (step-to-step sd 8–11 points, worst with 150 training clips), but a ridge probe fit on the same residual at every step is smooth and monotone. A dip followed by a recovery is a failed fit; information cannot return after a projection.
  • Cross-half generalisation: a direction probe trained on left-half patch tokens scores 0.81 (layer 1), 0.96 (layer 9) and 0.83 (layer 24) on right-half tokens.

Supplementary figures for these checks are in outputs/figures/report/supp_*.png.

Limits and next steps

  • Training-set-size sweep at each layer.
  • Acceleration vs constant velocity with distance travelled matched, using the mixed direction set.
  • Steering variants: disk tokens only, re-applied at every layer, or later layers.
  • Our protocol uses one split; paper-protocol steering varies 3.5–14° across splits.
  • Remaining differences from the paper: flat 2D disk vs shaded 3D sphere; 64 directions vs 8.
