Kinematics inside V-JEPA 2

Talk content, one card per slide. Each card: title, points, figure, and the takeaway line. Numbers are on held-out test clips unless stated.

1

Question

  • Does a frozen video world model (V-JEPA 2 ViT-L) represent the direction, speed and acceleration of a moving object?
  • Where, in how many dimensions, and can we steer it?
  • Part 1: reproduce Joseph et al. (probing, nullspace probing, subspace steering). Part 2: manifold (spline) steering after Goodfire.
Main result: the representation is easy to read and hard to control; manifold geometry matters for the circular variable.
2

Setup

  • Data: synthetic clips, one disk, 16 frames at 24 fps. 1,500 direction clips (64 angles), 1,536 speed (0.25–4 m/s), 1,536 acceleration (0.25–10 m/s²).
  • Model: vjepa2-vitl-fpc64-256, frozen. Features: residual stream after each of 24 blocks, averaged over tokens.
  • Splits 60/20/20 by label value. Probes and subspaces fit on train, hyper-parameters on val, numbers on test.
  • Labels checked against the disk's measured trajectory (32 px/m, direction within 1–2°).
3

Probing: all three variables are linearly readable from the first block

  • R² at layer 1: 0.85 direction, 0.98 speed, 0.98 acceleration. A randomly initialised encoder gets 0.83 / 0.93 / 0.91.
  • Pretraining shows in the error: direction 12° at layer 1 to 3° from layer 13; the random encoder stays at 11°.
  • Single spatial patches decode direction from layer 6 (median R² 0.95).
  • No step at the paper's layer 8. Their low early scores (0.22) appear here only with little data and an under-trained gradient probe (0.31 on 96 clips vs 0.63 for ridge).
Readability says little about pretraining on these clips; one attention block over the tokens already exposes the motion.
4

The acceleration probe reads displacement

  • Within each dataset the distance travelled is fixed by the label.
  • Applied to constant-velocity clips (true acceleration 0), the acceleration probe predicts 6.8 m/s² on average and tracks distance with r = 0.993.
  • The paper's datasets are built the same way.
"Acceleration is represented" is not established by this probe.
5

Nullspace probing: tens of dimensions per variable

  • At layer 12, R² falls below 0.3 after removing 58 (direction), 51 (speed), 48 (acceleration) dimensions.
  • Removing random directions does nothing; removing the top 4–13 principal components removes everything.
  • Paper protocol at its layer 8: median 60 direction probes (120 dims), 44 speed. Paper reports ≈44 and ≈28.
  • Redundancy drops sharply between layers 7 and 8.
6

The paper's sawtooth is a logging artefact, not a sine–cosine code

  • After a projection, the next probe can only see less information, so its best possible score cannot go up. A low probe followed by a high one means the low one failed to fit.
  • With the paper's optimiser, curves are jagged; a ridge probe on the same data is monotone.
  • Logging each probe twice (before and after removing its own directions) reproduces the exact period-2 pattern.
  • The paper's explanation needs one direction removed per step; its procedure removes both sin and cos at once.
7

Subspace steering works at the edited layer, then fades

  • Replace the activation's coordinates in the span of N probes so they read the target. With 10 probes the error to target reaches the probe's own error (4°).
  • Paper protocol: 8.4° at 20 probes (range 3.5–14° over splits); the paper reports 11.9°.
  • Read out at later layers, the edit is gone within 3 blocks: 4° → 25° → 45° → 73°, 85° at layer 24 (random edit: 90°).
Reading the same layer you edited is close to circular. This motivated the behavior test.
8

Manifolds: direction is a ring, speed and acceleration are curves

  • PCA to 64 dims; one centroid per label value; a curve through them parameterised by the label (periodic for direction).
  • Curve basis chosen on val: 4 harmonics for direction, 1–2 knots otherwise. The paper's interpolating cubic chases noise with 14 clips per value.
  • The ring is found without labels: the angle in the top-2 PCs of the centroids matches θ with r = 0.997.
9

Spline steering at the edited layer: same accuracy, large side effect

  • Both methods put 98–100% of clips on target, for seen and unseen target values.
  • Outside the fitted range the spline drops to ~50%; subspace steering stays at 99%.
  • Steering speed with the spline sets direction to chance (10% intact vs 94%): the paper's operation overwrites the whole top-64 PCA subspace, which also carries direction.
9b

Both spline failures are fixable

  • Outside the range: the fitted curve was clamped at its ends. Extending it linearly past the ends fixes the extrapolation.
  • Side effect: instead of replacing the top-64 PCA component, move the clip along the curve, x + s(target) − s(û), with û the clip's current value decoded by a probe. Everything else the clip encodes is kept.
  • Fixed spline: 96–100% on target for speed, 88–100% for acceleration; direction intact at the pre-steering rate (94% / 89%).
With these fixes, spline steering matches subspace steering at the edited layer.
10

Behavior test: judge steering by the model's own forecast

  • Behavior = the predictor's forecast of frames 9–16 from frames 1–8. Read out with a probe fit on unsteered forecasts only (94% / 97% / 87% on target for clean clips).
  • The encoder must see frames 1–8 only. Encoding the full clip (the Hugging Face default) leaks the true future into the context: swapping in a target clip's tokens then moves 16–31% of forecasts; with context-only encoding it moves 91–100%.
  • Four edits: subspace or spline, applied to the pooled average or to each token.
11

Steering reaches the forecast only near the top of the encoder

  • Edits at layers 4–16 move the forecast by at most a few points, for every method, including transplanting the target's full average activation.
  • Effects appear at layers 20–24.
Probe-level control at layer 12 is not control of the model.
12

The forecast depends on the token pattern, not the token average

  • Swap only a donor clip's token average: 0% of forecasts change (layers 0–20).
  • Swap only its pattern (each token minus the average): 100% take the donor's motion at layers 0–8.
  • Pooled probes and pooled steering operate on the average. The model reads the pattern.
13

Hitting a target: subspace steering at layer 24 is strongest

  • Strength tuned on val. On target: pooled subspace 94% / 62% / 93% (direction / speed / acceleration); pooled spline 47–51%.
  • Direction intact while steering speed: subspace 80%, per-token spline 83%, pooled spline 52% (90% before steering).
14

Paths: straight lines teleport, splines turn smoothly

  • Turn the forecast direction by 180° in 10 steps and check each intermediate forecast.
  • Straight paths: 4–10% of intermediate forecasts at the intended direction. The forecast stays at the start, then jumps.
  • Per-token spline path: 77%. Same edit, endpoints and subspace as the per-token straight path (7%); only the geometry differs.
  • For 90° turns, or for speed and acceleration, straight paths do about as well.
The Goodfire claim holds behaviorally where the geometry is non-trivial: a circular variable and a large turn.
15

Conclusions

  • Kinematics are linearly readable from the first block, even in a random encoder; readability is not evidence of use.
  • Each variable spans ~50–60 linear dimensions; the paper's sawtooth is an artefact.
  • Same-layer steering success is near-circular. Judged by the predictor, pooled edits below layer 20 do nothing, because the model reads the token pattern, not the average.
  • For a single target, subspace steering at the top of the encoder is strongest and most specific.
  • For moving along a circular variable, spline paths avoid teleportation; per-token splines work best.
16

Limitations and next steps

  • Simple synthetic stimulus: flat 2D disk, one object. Speed and acceleration are confounded with displacement.
  • Behavior readout is still a linear probe, on the predictor's output. It is fit only on unsteered forecasts.
  • Most steering evaluations use one split; paper-protocol steering varies 3.5–14° across splits.
  • Next: per-token steering restricted to disk tokens; distance-matched acceleration test; decoding the forecast into pixels.
